# Israel Flight Simulator: notes for Claude Code

A single-page Three.js (r128, global `THREE` from cdnjs) flight simulator over Israel. Plain classic scripts, no modules,
no bundler, no framework. Everything shares one global scope, so the load order in `index.html` matters.

## Commands

- Run: open `index.html` (or serve the folder).
- Single-file build: `python3 tools/build.py` → `dist/index.html`. Rebuild it after any change if `dist/` is kept in git.
- Test: `python3 tools/smoke_test.py` (needs `pip install playwright && playwright install chromium`). It loads the page
  headlessly, reports JS errors, steps the flight model in all three modes and writes `smoke.png`.
- Syntax check: `for f in src/*.js; do node --check $f; done`.
- Data: `python3 tools/make_estimate.py && python3 tools/ghsl2bld.py` (inputs in `raw/`, see README).
- Land cover, buildings, sites, railways: `python3 tools/osm2world.py` (same input, about 2 minutes).
- Roads: `python3 tools/osm2roads.py` (needs `pip install osmium` and `raw/israel_and_palestine.osm.pbf`, see README).

## World conventions

- 1 unit = 1 m, but the map is compressed 4:1 horizontally (elevation is real). `KX = 23750`, `KZ = 27700` world metres per
  degree. `lon2x`, `lat2z`, `x2lon`, `z2lat` convert. North = −Z, East = +X, Up = +Y.
- Scale in motion: aloft the aircraft crosses the map at a quarter of its true speed (`HSCALE`, `hsAt(ra)` blends smoothly, on a log scale
  of height, from full scale at 30 m to a quarter at 1,500 m, so a low jet does not seem to leap forward near the ground), so flight times and distances match the real country and climb and descent angles against the
  terrain are true. Anything that predicts a position from the velocity (terrain look-ahead, GPWS, turn radii) multiplies by `hsAt`.
  Distances shown to the player are real (`realDist`), never world metres. Runways, buildings and other objects are full size.
- The glide path is 3° in real terms; on the map it is curved (`gsHeight`, `gsDist`, tabulated from `hsAt`). `ilsOf().gs` is degrees
  above it in real terms.
- Map window: lon 33.6–36.4, lat 29.2–33.6. Terrain grid `NX × NZ` is 384 × 704 on desktop, 240 × 440 on touch devices
  (`HIRES`). The outer ring of vertices is stretched to the horizon.
- Aircraft body axes: nose −Z, right +X, up +Y. Attitude is the quaternion `S.q`; `readAttitude()` derives `S.pitch`,
  `S.bank` (right positive) and `S.hdg` (degrees) with Euler order `YXZ`.
- `renderer.logarithmicDepthBuffer` is on. Every custom `ShaderMaterial` must include the `logdepthbuf_*` chunks and set
  `material.extensions.fragDepth = true` (see `SHADER_VS`, `TERRAIN_FS`, `WATER_FS`).
- Three r128 quirk: `InstancedMesh.setColorAt` only tints if the material has `vertexColors: true` and the geometry has a
  white `color` attribute; and the colour buffer is sized by `count` at the first `setColorAt`, so call it before lowering `count`.

## Look and resolution

- Render resolution is dynamic (`RES`, `autoRes` in `src/02-scene-terrain.js`): it starts at the screen's pixel density (up to 1.75, touch 1.5), steps
  down by 0.25 while frames average over 27 ms and back up (to at most 2×) under 14.5 ms. `?res=1` in the address pins it (use it for screenshots).
- Free look (`LOOK`, `setupLook` in `src/06-flight.js`): dragging the view turns the head in the cockpit and wing views and swings the chase camera;
  it eases back when released. The flight deck is set low (glare shield, slim posts) so the world fills most of the cockpit view.
- The aircraft's ground shadow (`buildShadow`, updated in `updateCamera`): a blurred silhouette where the sun puts it, on the rendered mesh
  (`triHeight`), fading by 900 m of height and at night.
- Terrain shader: rows a few metres apart (orchards, vineyards, greenhouses, solar) fade out within about 1 km, or they shimmer; two extra
  grain layers sharpen the ground within 650 m. Building windows blend to an even tone beyond about 2 km. Road textures are 4× (128/256 px wide).
- In GLSL strings as in the JS around them: never put a `//` comment before code on the same line. It has swallowed code five times here.
- Atmosphere and tone (one look for every material): `HAZE_GLSL` (`hazeK`, `hazeCol`, `toneC` in `src/02-scene-terrain.js`) gives the terrain, water and sky
  shaders haze that thins with height, shifts blue with distance and warms toward a low sun, plus a soft highlight shoulder. Built-in materials get the
  same haze and shoulder through replaced `THREE.ShaderChunk` fog and tone-mapping chunks (set before any material compiles; `renderer.toneMapping` is
  `CustomToneMapping`). HUD-like sprites use `toneMapped: false`.
- Baked terrain light (`bakeTerrainLight`, attribute `aLit`): per vertex, in-sun (a shadow marched across `HGT` toward the sun, cached per time of day)
  and valley occlusion. The day sun is an afternoon sun, 32° up in the south-west, so relief and facades have a lit and a shaded side.
- Water: three fading swell trains on top of the noise, a sun path that widens toward the horizon, lines of surf by depth. Each wave train must fade
  before its wavelength reaches a pixel, or the horizon fills with rings.
- The airliner and business jet: one lathe `fuselage()` with a canvas livery (`liveryMat`: belly, cheatline, tail colour, a window per row, cockpit panes;
  a second canvas lights the windows at night). Lights are additive sprites (`AC.glow`), landing lights come on with the gear and throw a pool of light at
  night (`LPOOL`), wings flex with the load, paint reflects a 64 px cube picture of the sky taken once per time of day (`bakeSkyReflection`), and each
  type has its own ground-shadow silhouette, narrowed by bank.
- Camera (`CAMX` in `updateCamera`): the chase camera leans into turns (half as much in Easy) and looks into them; a new start swings in from the side;
  a change of view blends over 0.38 s; shake is summed sines; `setViewOffset` keeps the aircraft clear of the instruments, and of the menu sheet
  (`menuOffset`).
- Round three of the look (colour, sky, cities):
  - One final grade lives in `toneC`, in the replaced tone-mapping chunk and at the end of the sky shader; keep the three copies identical.
  - Sky dome: cirrus streaks (`uCir` per time of day), round stars, a moon with a halo at night (the night light direction is the moon). `TOD` holds the
    colour script: an orange sunset over violet shade, a moonlit blue night; `den` is the haze density per time of day (denser than real air, for the 4:1 map).
  - Clouds are one instanced, shaded billboard batch (`cloudMat`, uniforms `uCol`, `uOp`, `uGrow`, `uDrift`); in a storm they swell into a dark deck.
    Rain is streaks in a box round the camera (`updateRain`). The explosion has a flash, debris that bounces, a scorch mark and long smoke.
  - Buildings: `windowize` reflects the sky in every pane, flashes the sun pane by pane, colours lit windows three ways at night and lights the lowest
    floors from the street. Buildings cast shadows on the ground through a per-cell box texture sampled in `TERRAIN_FS` (`tBS`, desktop only), not a shadow
    map. Blocks are tested with `obstBox` before they are placed and dense cells stand their blocks side by side, so nothing runs through anything else.
    City palettes are regional (`palAt`: stone in the hills, white and glass on the coast, sand in the Negev), walls are scaled to 0.88.
  - Urban ground near the camera has yards, car parks, pavements and dashed streets; forests have a sun-lit canopy; rock and sand take half their colour
    from the ground around; the desert varies in hue, not in grey. Trees stand on a soft shadow disc. At night roads are red and white ribbons
    (`updateTraffic`), cities glow from altitude, point lights are clamped on screen (`clampPoints`).
  - The fighter is a lathe model with an intake, a clear canopy and pilot, missiles and an afterburner with shock diamonds; the flight deck is a room
    (floor, seats, overhead; a canopy bow and nose for bubble canopies); the terminal has windowed walls, a curved roof, piers, an apron texture, a tower.
  - A closed power lever means no power in every level: the aircraft glides down (Medium's protections do not add power behind the pilot's back).
  - Shake is small and almost absent in the cockpit and wing views (a metre from the panel, centimetres of camera movement made everything jump).
- Views (`CAMS`, `S.cam`, the C key or the View button cycles, the settings row lists them): 0 cockpit, 1 chase, 2 wing, 3 fly-by (a camera standing in the
  world ahead, with a long lens, that moves on after the aircraft has passed), 4 above, 5 front (looking back at the aircraft), 6 below (under the belly,
  looking down and ahead), 7 tail (the top of the fin). Views 0, 2, 6, 7 are fixed to the aircraft and take free look.
- Aircraft detail: turbofans are lathe nacelles with a chrome lip, a fan that turns (`M.fans`, `fanMat`), a duct and a core nozzle, on swept pylons; gear
  legs have oleos, torque links, hubs and doors and the wheels turn on the ground (`M.wheels`); spoilers rise on the brake (`M.spoil`); the aerobatic
  plane is a lathe with a painted sweep, a sunburst on the wings, three blades that give way to a blurred disc (`M.blades`, `M.disc`) and a pilot under
  a clear canopy; the fighter carries a drop tank, pylons and formation-light strips.
- The game must not end by accident (`guardBack`, `saveFlight`, `savedFlight` in `src/08-app.js`): Back in flight opens the menu and Back again resumes
  (a history entry is kept in front of the page; from the start screen Back leaves as usual); leaving asks first; pull-to-refresh, the long-press menu
  and page pinch are off; the flight is saved every 5 s and the start screen offers "Continue the last flight" for 45 minutes.
- Interface polish layer (the last block of CSS in `index.html`): tokens (`--r1`, `--r2`, `--glass`, `--glass-solid`, `--elev`, `--ease`), a Hebrew font
  stack with no letter-spacing, menus as a side sheet (a bottom sheet on an upright phone) so the world shows, aircraft cards with pictures rendered at
  start-up (`shootPlanes`), a one-line title with the aircraft glyph, a dawn loading screen (`body:not(.ready)`), entry animations, one message slot
  (`body.toasting` hides the hint), the readout strip in cells, a redesigned stick and power lever, `body.paused` / `body.crashed` hiding the HUD.

- Round four (the proposals that had been left out):
  - Post pass (`POST`, `renderFrame` in `src/02-scene-terrain.js`): the frame goes to a multisampled target, bright parts are blurred at 1/4 and 1/8 size and
    added back, with a light sharpen. `POST.k` is 0.28 by day and 1 at night (white paint in the sun must not glow). `?post=0` turns it off, and it turns
    itself off on a device that stays slow at the lowest resolution. Screenshots of aircraft cards (`shootPlanes`) render directly, not through it.
  - Graphics setting (`applyGfx`, `SAVE.gfx`): automatic, fast (1 pixel per pixel, no post pass), best (full density, pinned).
  - Time that passes (`TODX`, `todAt`, `todTick` in `src/04-sky.js`, the fourth "time of day"): afternoon, sunset, dusk, night, dawn, about eleven minutes
    round. `TODC` is the set of values in force (use it, not `TOD[S.tod]`). Terrain shadows for each step are baked a few rows per frame (`bakeTerrainRows`).
  - The aircraft shadows itself through one small shadow map that follows it (larger screens only; `?shadow=0` turns it off). Nothing else casts into it.
  - Lone city blocks turn to face the nearest road (`roadDirAt`); blocks that share a cell stay on the lattice. Jerusalem's Old City has its real wall
    outline (`OLDCITY`, `inOldCity`) and a dense fabric of small stone houses (`buildOldCity`); generic blocks and modern sites keep out of it.
  - Terrain shader: the sun flashes off solar fields and greenhouses (`lcG`), ground is wet and mirrors the sky in a storm (`uWet`), Hermon's snow lies
    lower on north slopes. Freighters have a shaped hull, containers and a foam wake; Tel Aviv's breakwaters are rock mounds.
  - Cars of the traffic within 650 m of a low camera are drawn as small 3D cars on the right-hand lane (the light dots ride 3.8 m over the ground, the road
    surface 2.2 to 3 m: take heights from the dots, not from `triHeight`).
  - A game controller works with nothing on screen: left stick, triggers or right stick for power, A view, B brake, X land, Y gear, LB flaps, Start menu.
  - At night the instruments dim (`body.night`); the key guide shows keycaps.

## Start-up

`init()` (in `src/08-app.js`) runs as soon as the scripts are in and only builds the choice screen: nothing in it may need the world. `buildWorld()` then
builds the world one builder at a time with a breath between them, behind that screen (`body.building`: dawn sky, a running line under the title). Before
`WORLD.ready` a choice on the screen is only kept (`PL`, `START`); FLY pressed early sets `WORLD.wantFly` and the flight starts when the world is there.
`?hold=3000` delays the build, for testing that state. Test scripts wait for `WORLD.ready`, not for the FLY button.

## Canvas textures survive a GPU reset

Android browsers can wipe 2D canvases after a GPU reset or while the tab sleeps, which used to leave a black minimap and invisible
place names. `setupCanvasGuard` (in `src/08-app.js`) saves every canvas used as a texture, and the baked map image, as a PNG at start-up
and repaints them on `contextrestored`, on `webglcontextrestored`, when the page comes back into view, and when a reference pixel of
the map image changes (checked every 3 s). A canvas texture created after start-up is not covered: create textures during `init`.

## Menus and interface standard

- The start screen and the pause menu are built by one function, `renderPanel('start' | 'pause')` (in `src/08-app.js`): the same folded
  sections in the same order (aircraft, difficulty, take off from a runway `TAKEOFFS`, start in the air `AIRSTARTS` (led by "choose any place on the map", `pickButton`), practise a landing
  `LANDTESTS`, settings, keyboard keys on desktop), each heading showing its current choice, and a sticky footer with a summary and
  the one main button (FLY / CONTINUE). Aircraft and difficulty are open by default; open/closed state is kept in `OPEN`.
- On the start screen a choice only selects (`START`); in the pause menu a start point or aircraft starts at once.
- Settings are rows of option buttons (`settingsRows`, `aria-pressed`), every choice visible; never a button that cycles.
- The level chip in flight opens the menu at the difficulty levels; only the H key switches level directly.
- The screen is kept on while flying (`keepAwake`: the Screen Wake Lock API, or a tiny muted video where it is missing).
- Touch targets are at least 44 px; icon buttons get localized `aria-label`s in `applyLang`, which also sets `<html lang>`.
- Every user-facing string is bilingual (`L('English', 'עברית')`, or `TRD` for data labels). Aviation instruments (PFD, ft, kt) stay as they are.
- Units are the player's choice (settings; `SAVE.uSpd`, `uDist`, `uAlt`): speed kt / km/h / mph, distance km / miles / nautical miles, height
  ft / m. Everything outside the PFD goes through `spdTxt`, `kmTxt`, `altTxt` (`src/07-hud-and-modes.js`); never print a raw unit. The speed strip
  shows speed, altitude, height above the ground (`b.agl`) and heading; the PFD shows `AGL` at every height.
- No credits or attribution lines anywhere in the game (the owner's decision); the data sources are credited in `README.md`.
- Takeoffs: one per airport; `rev: true` starts from the far end of the runway, towards open ground (Haifa, Rosh Pina, Beer Sheva).
- On touch, Land, Roll and Loop are folded behind one "⋯" button (`cMore`, `ctxMore`, `foldMore`): it opens them, and they fold away after a choice or 7 s.
  The button is magenta during an automatic landing (cancel is inside). Only View, and in Expert gear, flaps and brake, stay out.
- Touch steering (`setupTouch` in `src/08-app.js`; chosen after looking at how the mobile simulators and action flying games do it):
  - The stick floats. The lower left of the screen (`#stZone`: 46% × 68%, on an upright phone 62% × 46%) is the stick's zone; it appears under the thumb
    wherever that lands, its base follows a thumb that runs past the rim, there is a 7% dead zone rescaled so nothing is lost, and the response is curved
    (power 1.45). At rest `#stick` is only a faint ring and takes no touches. The zone takes the drags there, so free look is on the rest of the screen.
  - The power lever is dragged from where it stands (a touch alone changes nothing; a tap still sets it), has a touch area wider than it looks, and ticks
    at idle and full power.
  - Tilt steering is an option in the settings on touch devices (`SAVE.tilt`, `TILT`, `tiltStart` in `src/07c-extras.js`): gravity from `devicemotion`
    in screen axes, neutral taken when a flight starts or the menu closes, 30° of roll and 22° of pitch for full deflection, always yoke-like. The
    permission iOS needs is asked from the settings tap or the FLY tap. The maths passes a synthetic test for both platforms' sign conventions, but it
    has never run on a real phone.
- HUD buttons use `tapButton` (acts on `pointerup`): a click is only sent for a one-finger tap, so with a thumb on the stick nothing else worked.
- Keys: while the menu is open only Esc is the game's; Ctrl/Alt/Meta with a letter is left to the browser; leaving in mid-flight asks first.
- `SAVE` keeps plane, level, language, stick sense, the picked place, time, weather, view, names, smoke, key guide, sound, instruments (`pfd`) ; the loader validates every field (a damaged save must not stop the game). A first visit starts in the air over Tel Aviv.
- Keep the screen quiet (the owner's rule: hide what is not needed now, never remove a function). In cruise only the speed strip, the map, the
  menu button and the level chip show. The way to the nearest airport appears for 7 s on coming into range and when low and near; gear and
  flap chips and rows only when low, in Expert or while landing; the autopilot row only when it is on; status hints ("holding 2,000 ft") are gone,
  only instructions remain, and the autoland and autopilot hints show for 9 s; the key guide shows for the first 45 s of a flight unless pinned
  (`legendOn`, `SAVE.legendPin`). There is no passport, score or credits section in the menus.
- Easy mode climbs over rising ground by itself when low (`arcadeStep`), so letting go never flies into a hill; flat ground can still be landed on.

## Flight model

Point mass with lift, drag, thrust and weight, rate-command rotation (not a moment model). The atmosphere is ISA (`isa(h)`: density and
speed of sound, troposphere and lower stratosphere). Drag: parabolic polar + gear, flaps, spoilers, an overspeed term past `vmo` (an
indicated-airspeed limit) and compressibility (`waveDrag`: a wall past `mcr` for subsonic jets, a transonic hump for the fighter). Thrust
lapses with speed (`lapse`) and density (`rhoExp`: 0.7 jets, 1.7 the piston engine); engines spool at `spool` per second. `nmax` caps the
load factor in Expert. Validated against real types (sea level unless stated):

| | Stall clean | Max speed | 10 km | Climb | Ceiling | g limit | Roll | Real reference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| airliner | 142 kt | 385 kt | M0.80 | 4,900 fpm | 43,000 ft | 2.5 | ~25°/s | A320: 145 kt, VMO 350 kt, MMO 0.82, 39,800 ft, 2.5 g |
| bizjet | 106 kt | 356 kt | M0.76 | 5,200 fpm | 47,600 ft | 3 | ~45°/s | mid-size jet: ~100 kt, VMO ~320 kt, M0.8, 45,000 ft |
| fighter | 118 kt | M1.07 | M1.49 | 44,600 fpm | 64,800 ft | 9 | ~250°/s | F-16C: ~125 kt, M1.2 low, M2 high, 50,000 fpm, 9 g |
| stunt | 58 kt | 187 kt | – | 2,600 fpm | 21,000 ft | 10 | ~330°/s | Extra 300: 55 kt, 185 kt, 3,200 fpm, 16,000 ft, ±10 g |
| concorde | 135 kt (no flaps) | 540 kt | M1.43; M2.04 at 15–18 km | 17,000 fpm | 59,400 ft | 2.5 | ~30°/s | Concorde: lands at 162 kt, M2.04, 60,000 ft |
| stealth | 114 kt (no flaps) | 538 kt | M0.90 | 8,200 fpm | 44,000 ft | 6 | ~125°/s | F-117: lands at about 150 kt, M0.92, 45,000 ft, 6 g |

The three added types: `concorde` (155 t, a nose that droops with the gear `M.droop`, four reheat flames, `wave` = its own supersonic drag, `mmo` = Mach limit,
a negative `lapse` and `rhoExp` 0.55 for intakes that gain thrust with speed and height; thrust lapse is clamped to 0.35–1.2), `stealth` (an F-117 built only
of flat facets from a vertex table `V`, DoubleSide so it takes no self-shadow; subsonic, no afterburner, no flaps), `bomber` (a B-52: eight engines in four
pods, wings that droop on the ground and bow up with lift through the fourth and fifth entries of `M.wings`, outrigger wheels `M.outrig`). The analytical
check lives in the scratch script the numbers came from; redo it after changing them. A fast, flapless approach captures the final only when nearer the
centreline (the cone in `updateControls` narrows with speed).

Re-run these numbers after changing `PLANES` (the analytical part is a few lines over `isa`, `waveDrag` and the polar).

## Flight modes (`S.mode`, set only through `setMode`)

- 0 Easy (`S.arc`): `arcadeStep` replaces the physics. Speed changes come from the real thrust and drag (`arcadeAccel`, with gear and flap drag), so take-off runs and
  rollouts are realistic (airliner: lift-off after about 1.1 km and 26 s, touchdown at about 133 kt, stops in about 700 m). The slowest speed
  `arcVmin()` follows the automatic flaps. After landing with the power at idle the brakes come on by themselves. The stick steers the flight path directly, the lever sets speed,
  release flies straight and level, there is an automatic flare near the ground. Holding the stick down low slows to landing speed and floats
  until slow, so that alone lands it. Easy does crash (the owner's decision): water, a hillside, a steep hit, buildings, other aircraft. Only a
  too-fast touchdown skips back into the air with a message. Each aircraft has a ceiling (`aCeil`), and it cannot climb at idle (`gmax` follows the lever).
- 1 Medium (`S.assist`): real physics with flight-path hold, automatic gear and flaps, stall and terrain protection.
- 2 Expert: real physics, rate command with flight-path hold when the stick is released, manual gear and flaps.
- Autoland (`S.land`) and the autopilot (`S.ap.on`) always run on the physics path, also from Easy mode.
- Automatic landing (L key / Land button) starts anywhere within `LAND_RANGE` (40 km, real) of the nearest runway with an ILS (`landTarget`).
  Phases in `S.landPh`: `intercept` (fly to an entry point on the extended centreline, or, with `S.landJoin`, a localizer intercept
  onto it, glide-path height, terrain clearance), `final` (the ILS law, captured when lined up and not more than 5° high; going round
  if far too high, too fast or not over the runway close in), `rollout` (after touchdown, also through a bounce). The localizer gain divides by
  `hsAt` (ground speed on the map is scaled), the flare is flown on the flight path, obstacles (`obstacleTop`) count in the clearance height, and
  after 3 failed tries or 10 minutes near the field it gives up with a message (`S.landTries`, `S.landT`). 45 of 45 random starts land within
  10 m of the centreline; the airliner at Masada (a 1,200 m strip beside a 370 m rock) usually gives up.
- `landPanel` (in `src/07-hud-and-modes.js`) drives the landing panel under the speed strip and the touch button states: Land is
  green when an airport is in range, dimmed when not (tapping says where the nearest one is), magenta while landing; the chips say
  whether gear and flaps are automatic (Easy, Medium, autoland) or manual (Expert, keys G and F).
- Touchdown limits (`physics`): sink 9.5 m/s autoland, 7.5 Medium, 5.5 Expert jets, 6.5 Expert fighter and stunt; over 2 × Vs0 is a crash anywhere,
  off a field over max(45 m/s, 1.3 × Vs0). The reason shown is the most violent that applies. Never name a local `L` or `D` in code that calls `L()`.
- The edge of the map: a warning 5 km (real) inside it; beyond it Easy and Medium turn back by themselves (`updateSystems`), Expert is only told.
- Pitch input is positive = nose up in every mode; `SAVE.inv` flips it for people who want a real stick.

## Roads

`tools/osm2roads.py` joins OSM ways into chains, keeps one line per divided road (only the parts of a one-way chain that run beside an
opposite, parallel kept line of the same class are dropped, so a road never loses a stretch) and flags it `dual`. Loose chain ends are
joined to the network: straight to a road within 60 m, or along tertiary roads and links up to 3 km. `OSM_ROADS` entries are
[class, route number, delta-encoded lon/lat, dual].

`roadLines()` (in `src/03-world.js`) turns `OSM_ROADS` into world polylines with points at most 45 m apart: class 2 motorway and trunk,
1 primary, 0 secondary. Half-widths come from `roadHW(cls, dual)` (real widths: single carriageway 7 / 10 / 12 m, divided 16 / 18 / 22 m, since
buildings are full size). Divided roads use the two-carriageway texture with a median, single ones the two-lane texture (many trunk roads
are single). `roadRank` lifts bigger roads slightly so smaller ones pass under them, and `roadJunctions` lays a plain asphalt patch on every
level crossing and T-junction (on the through road, at its width) so markings do not cross; a road crossing a motorway is a bridge, no patch.
The hand-drawn `ROADS` (spline through waypoints, with a gentle random wander) only fill in
the stretches the OSM extract does not cover (Jordan, Lebanon, Sinai). Route shields go on numbered roads longer than about 7 km.

## Airfields

Runways are at real scale while the map around them is compressed 4:1, so `ap.R` (the circle the ground is levelled in, and where
touching down counts as on the field) covers several real towns. `airfieldAt(x, z, pad)` is the airfield proper (runways with overruns,
parallel taxiway, apron): buildings, roads, rails and trees keep off that, not off the circle.

## Keeping roads clear

`nearRoad(x, z, r)` (in `src/03-world.js`) is true when a round object of radius `r` would touch a road or railway. It uses an index of the
same centreline splines the road and rail meshes are built from (`ROADS`, `RAIL_LINES`). Every placement routine (city buildings, houses, trees,
ponds, pivot circles, Nahalal, minarets, heliostats, turbines) checks it, so call it for anything new that is placed on the ground.

## Minimap and names

`drawMap` sizes the map canvas to its on-screen size × devicePixelRatio and sizes text in CSS pixels. `mapLabels` places labels by priority
(waypoints, cities of 100k+, motorway numbers, smaller towns, regional road numbers) and drops any that would overlap; the layout is cached
per size, zoom and language. On the enlarged map, two fingers or the mouse wheel zoom 1–8× (`MV`, `mapZoomAt`), one finger or
the mouse pans, double-click zooms in, and a tap (not a drag, see `mapWasDragged`) opens and closes it. Names in the sky (`buildLabels`, `updateLabels`): only towns of 80,000 people and more (38 of them), seen from 8–20 km of game world; at most four at once, the
ones ahead along the direction of flight (within 65° of it, or right below), nearer, straighter ahead and bigger first; a name that would overlap one already shown is left out.

## Sites: what each model may look like

- No domes on Jewish buildings: a dome reads as a mosque. Synagogues and yeshivas are halls (raised roof, tall windows); only the 39
  largest OSM synagogues (1,500 m²+) are shown. Domes belong to mosques, churches that have them, the observatory.
- OSM `man_made=tower` is split by `tower:type`: minaret (dropped beside a mosque model, which has its own), bell tower, lookout
  (observation, watch, defensive), radio tower. Only `leisure=stadium` of 110 m and more get the stadium model (OSM tags small grounds too).
- Stadium geometry is shared (`LG.stands`, `wall`, `roof`, `roof2`, `rim`, `pitch` in `lmkGeo`): open canopies only (a closed cylinder
  segment draws a pie-slice cap over the pitch), and the pitch is thick enough to stay above uneven ground.

## Land cover and buildings from OpenStreetMap

- `LCR` / `lcAt(x, z)`: one land-cover class per 60 m cell (`LCN`: forest, orchard, vineyard, fields, parks, scrub, sand, rock, water,
  wetland, residential, industrial, greenhouses, solar, military, cemetery, construction). The terrain shader samples it (`tLC`) and
  paints each class; small ponds, basins and parks are left out by the tool (they would be 240 m blots). Trees follow it in `updateScatter`.
- Sites: besides towers, mosques, churches and the like, `tools/osm2world.py` now emits schools, hotels, railway stations, football pitches, courts, public
  pools, fuel stations, supermarkets, civic halls (town hall, community centre, library, theatre, museum, police, fire), monuments, barns and cowsheds,
  warehouses, sewage works, and buildings tagged 25 m and taller (`MINA` holds the smallest footprints). About 24,000 are placed (it was about 7,000);
  each kind has a model in `siteModels` and a `far` distance in `buildSites` (small things only draw near). Synagogues from 450 m² are shown.
- A cell with OpenStreetMap buildings but no satellite pixel is built from two buildings and 1.2% cover, four buildings, or 3% cover (it used to take
  eight buildings or 5%), so small villages and farmyards stand where they are mapped.
- `BOR`: per cell the building cover, dominant OSM building class and tallest tagged height. `buildCities` combines it with the GHSL raster:
  industrial → flat sheds (`flat`), greenhouses → rows of tunnels, apartments vs houses as mapped, dense cells get 2–3 blocks, tagged heights
  replace the average, and cells with OSM buildings but no GHSL pixel (newer neighbourhoods) are built. Buildings of 40 m+ are sites.
- Every strip mesh (roads, rails, rivers) must be wound to face up: `I.push(prev, prev + 1, k, prev + 1, k + 1, k)`. With DoubleSide a
  downward winding renders the underside, lit from below, and the strip looks black.

## Settlement data

One pixel of `BLD_PNG` is one 60 m game block (240 m of real ground): R height in metres, G style in bits 0–2
(1 apartment block, 2 stone, 3 red-roofed houses, 4 glass tower) and footprint size in bits 3–4, B random seed, A present.
`buildCities` places one building (or a few houses) per pixel, in 6 km buckets culled by distance to the bucket's nearest edge (not its centre), and registers each
box in the obstacle hash `OBST` for collisions. A block that touches a road is nudged off it or shrunk before it is dropped. Localities in `LOCS`
with no raster pixels nearby get a small cluster of red-roofed houses. `urbanAt` feeds the terrain shader's street grid, which is aligned to the
same 60 m lattice (`uOrg`).

## Known gaps and ideas

- The GHSL tile R6_C22 ends near latitude 33.1; north of it the raster is an estimate. Adding tile R5_C22 needs
  `tools/ghsl2bld.py` to mosaic two tiles.
- GHSL height is a 100 m average, so individual towers are invented (about 10% of the tallest cells).
- Never measured on a real phone. Roughly 77,000 building instances; if it stutters, lower the `far` distances in
  `buildCities` or raise the coverage threshold `TH` in `tools/ghsl2bld.py`.
- Terrain is formula-based (tables and Gaussian peaks in `rawHeight`), not a DEM. A real DEM could replace `rawHeight`
  through the same kind of embedded raster.
- Audio has only been checked for errors, never listened to.
- No licence file yet.

## Working style for this project

- Keep it dependency-free at runtime: one CDN script, everything else procedural or embedded.
- After changing flight laws, run the smoke test; it catches crashes in level flight and at take-off in all modes.
- User-facing text: Hebrew is the default language; add both strings with `L('English', 'עברית')`.
